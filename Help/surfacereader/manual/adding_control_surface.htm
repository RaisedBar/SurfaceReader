Adding a control surface for Windows

The following steps describe how to add a control surface to be monitored by SurfaceReader under Windows:

Step 1 - Installing Virtual MIDI Ports
  1. Install your virtual MIDI cable software onto your Windows computer (for example, MIDI-Yoke or LoopBe30 – see the System Requirements section for details).
  2. Configure the virtual MIDI cable software to provide the correct number of virtual cables.  You’ll need two virtual cables for each physical control surface that you want to use with SurfaceReader.  Please read the documentation that came with your software to help you configure it correctly.
Step 2 - Adding a Control Surface to an Application.
  1. Please check the documentation for your application and your control surface for instructions on how to configure your device to work with your audio/music application.  It’s a good idea to perform an initial configuration using the physical MIDI In and Out ports that your control surface is actually attached to.  You can now check that the device is working correctly before proceeding.
  2. Once you’re satisfied that the device and your application are working together correctly, modify the configuration to use a pair of your virtual MIDI cables instead of the actual MIDI ports you specified initially.  For example, if this is the first control surface you’ve installed, set the MIDI In to MIDI-Yoke In 1 and the MIDI Out to point to MIDI-Yoke Out 2.  Make a note of these settings, as they are vital for the next step.
  3. Close your application program.
Step 3 - Adding a Control Surface to SurfaceReader.

SurfaceReader comes supplied with surface protocol definitions for a number of popular devices, so there’s a good chance that there’s already built-in support for your hardware.  To add a control surface to SurfaceReader, follow these steps:

  1. Go to the Surfaces menu.
  2. Choose Add…
  3. In the dialog box that appears:
  4. Type a descriptive name into the Surface name: field.
  5. Press Tab to get to the list of available protocols then use your up/down arrow keys to highlight the protocol that your surface is using.  If you don’t see the protocol that you’re looking for, press Escape to exit the dialog box then read the Advanced Configuration section of this guide for details on how to define a custom protocol.
  6. Press Tab to get to the Hardware MIDI Setup button and press Enter to activate it.  This will open up another dialog that allows you to specify the MIDI In and Out ports that will handle the messages transmitted by your control surface’s buttons, etc..  When the dialog box first opens you will be positioned on the MIDI In list, so use your up/down arrow keys to highlight the MIDI In port that your surface is physically attached to.
  7. Press Tab to get to the MIDI Out list and choose the virtual MIDI Out port that represents the other end of the cable you used as the MIDI In within your application (for example, MIDI-Yoke Out 1 if your software’s MIDI In is set to MIDI-Yoke In 1).  This virtual port will pass your surface’s MIDI messages along to your application software.
  8. Press Tab until you get to the OK button and press Enter to leave the dialog box.
  9. Press Tab to get to the Display MIDI Setup button and press Enter to activate it.  This will open up a dialog box that allows you to specify the MIDI In and Out ports that will handle the messages transmitted by your application software back to your control surface.  When the dialog box first opens you will be positioned on the MIDI In list, so use your up/down arrow keys to highlight the virtual MIDI In port that represents the other end of the virtual cable your software is using as its MIDI Out (for example, highlight MIDI-Yoke In 2 if your application’s MIDI Out is set to MIDI-Yoke Out 2).  Press Tab to get to the MIDI Out list and highlight the MIDI Out port that your control surface is physically attached to.  This output port will be used to pass your application’s MIDI messages back to the display elements of your control surface (i.e. its LED lamps and/or text displays).
  10. Press Tab until you get to the OK button and press Enter to leave the dialog box.
  11. Press Tab to get to the Finish button and press Enter.  You should now see your surface’s descriptive name appear in the Surfaces list.
  12. Leave SurfaceReader open and load your audio/music application program.
  13. Check that your control surface still controls your application correctly – if your surface has motorised faders and they move as you load your application then you’re almost certainly set up correctly.  If things don’t appear to be working, you’ve almost certainly made a mistake in configuring the MIDI ports, either in your application’s configuration dialog, or within the Hardware or Display MIDI Setup dialogs within SurfaceReader, so double-check these instructions against your settings.

SurfaceReader saves your MIDI configuration, so you shouldn’t need to go through this process again unless your installed MIDI devices change.

Adding a control surface for Mac

The following steps describe how to add a control surface to be monitored by SurfaceReader under Mac OSX 10.6 (Snow Leopard), or later:

Adding a Control Surface to SurfaceReader.

SurfaceReader comes supplied with surface protocol definitions for a number of popular devices, so there’s a good chance that there’s already built-in support for your hardware.  To add a control surface to SurfaceReader, follow these steps:

  1. Go to the Surfaces menu.
  2. Choose Add…
  3. In the dialog box that appears:
  4. Type a descriptive name into the Surface name: field.
  5. Press Tab to get to the list of available protocols then select the protocol that your surface is using from the table.  If you don’t see the protocol that you’re looking for, press Escape to exit the dialog box then read the Advanced Configuration section of this guide for details on how to define a custom protocol.
  6. Press Tab to get to the Hardware MIDI Setup button and press Enter to activate it.  This will open up another dialog that allows you to specify the MIDI In port that will handle the messages transmitted by your control surface’s buttons, etc..  Select the MIDI In port that your surface is physically attached to from the table.
  7. Press Tab to get to the MIDI Out list and choose the virtual MIDI Out port that represents the other end of the cable you used as the MIDI In within your application (for example, MIDI-Yoke Out 1 if your software’s MIDI In is set to MIDI-Yoke In 1).  This virtual port will pass your surface’s MIDI messages along to your application software.
  8. Press Tab until you get to the OK button and press Enter to leave the dialog box.
  9. Press Tab to get to the Display MIDI Setup button and press Enter to activate it.  This will open up a dialog box that allows you to specify the MIDI Out port that will handle the messages transmitted by your application software back to your control surface in order to change display elements like text displays and LED lamps.  Select the MIDI Out port that your control surface is connected to from the table.
  10. Press Tab until you get to the OK button and press Enter to leave the dialog box.
  11. Press Tab to get to the Finish button and press Enter.  You should now see your surface’s descriptive name appear in the Surfaces list.
  12. Leave SurfaceReader open and load your audio/music application program.
  13. Load your audio application and follow the instructions in its documentation for setting up a control surface. However, instead of setting its MIDI In and Out port settings to the names of the physical ports that the surface is connected to, you should see additional ports with the same descriptive name that you used when you added your surface to SurfaceReader. For example, if you added a surface and named it "My BCF2000", you should find a MIDI In port called "My BCF2000 Hardware" and a MIDI Out port called "My BCF2000 Display". These are the ports you need to choose when using SurfaceReader.

SurfaceReader saves your MIDI configuration, so you shouldn’t need to go through this process again unless your installed MIDI devices change.
